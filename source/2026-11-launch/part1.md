**Quick gist:** On 2 November 2026 the UK government confirmed its "earned settlement" plan. For most migrants, the wait for permanent residence (ILR) moves from 5 years to 10. A few groups get faster routes. The rules haven't been laid in Parliament yet, so nothing has changed in law today, but the direction is now clear.

If you came to the UK on a Skilled Worker or Health and Care visa thinking "five years and I'm settled", this is the news you've been dreading. Let's break it down without the panic.

## What exactly was announced?

The Home Secretary set out the final shape of the new system in Parliament on 2 November. In the government's own words, the right to stay forever "will no longer be granted to migrants almost automatically after 5 years". The new default is **10 years** for people who are working, paying in and following the rules.

There are faster and slower lanes:

- **Faster:** the highest earners can settle in as little as 3 years. People earning above about £50,000, some public service workers, relatives of British citizens and Hong Kong BN(O) holders keep a 5-year route.
- **Default:** 10 years for most workers.
- **Slower:** people who have breached immigration rules or entered illegally face much longer waits, 20 years or more.

## The new must-haves for everyone

According to the Migration Observatory's breakdown, every settlement applicant will need to tick these boxes:

1. **English at B2 level**, up from B1.
2. **No debts owed to the government** and a clean record. That now includes cautions and court fines.
3. **Pass the Life in the UK test**, as before.
4. **Minimum earnings** of around £12,571 a year for several years. Couples can meet this jointly.

That English requirement is the one to start working on now. B2 is upper-intermediate. Many Nigerians will pass it comfortably, but get your certificate sorted early so it isn't a last-minute scramble.

## I'm already in the UK on a 5-year route. Am I affected?

This is the big question, and the honest answer is: **mostly yes, with some protections.** The government has said no one will be forced to leave. One protection that has been confirmed is for care workers who arrived before 12 May 2025: they can still settle after 5 years if their employer followed the rules and paid at least £25,000.

What you should know:

- **Don't switch visas without advice.** Under the new plans, the qualifying period has to be completed on your current route, so switching can restart your clock.
- **Partners and children settle with you** if they came with you. Children aged 18–24 who are still your dependants can settle alongside you too.
- **Watch the dates.** Legal firms tracking the plan say the rule changes are due to start being laid from December 2026 and roll out through 2027. Until they're in the Immigration Rules, the current 5-year rules still apply.

## What to do this week

- **Count your days.** Work out exactly when you'd hit 5 years under today's rules. If it's close, talk to a regulated adviser (OISC-registered or a solicitor) about whether you can apply before the changes start.
- **Book your English test** if you don't already have B2 or a UK degree that counts.
- **Keep your record spotless.** Pay any council tax arrears, parking fines or HMRC debts. Small debts can now cost you years.
- **Keep payslips and P60s.** You'll need to show earnings over several years.

## The bigger picture

The Home Office's own figures suggest about 500,000 people who would have settled between 2026 and 2030 will now wait longer. That's a lot of families. Expect immigration advisers to be very busy over the coming months, so if you need help, book early.

**Quick gist:** From 1 January 2027 the standard UK Graduate visa drops from 2 years to 18 months. But the UK Budget on 28 October 2026 said graduates of "high-compliance universities with strong labour market outcomes" will keep 2 years. The list of universities hasn't been published yet. If you can apply on or before 31 December 2026, you still get the full 2 years.

Nigerians are among the biggest users of the Graduate route. Vanguard reported that Nigerian nationals received over 20,000 Graduate route approvals in the period it reviewed, making them the third-largest group. So this one affects a lot of us.

## What changed, step by step

1. **Earlier this year** the government announced that the Graduate visa for bachelor's and master's graduates would fall from 2 years to **18 months** for applications from 1 January 2027.
2. **On 28 October 2026** the Budget softened that. It said the visa "will be set at two years for individuals who graduate from high-compliance universities where there are strong labour market outcomes".
3. **PhD graduates** still get 3 years either way.

So in 2027 there will effectively be two tiers: 2 years for some universities and 18 months for the rest.

## Which universities count as "high-compliance"?

Nobody knows yet. The government hasn't published the list or the exact test. "High compliance" usually refers to how well a university does on Home Office checks, such as visa refusal rates and how many students complete their courses. "Strong labour market outcomes" suggests graduate earnings and job data will matter too.

Until the list is out, don't assume your university is on it.

## What it means for you

**If you finish your course this year:** you need to be in the UK on your Student visa and your university must have reported your successful completion. If you can apply **on or before 31 December 2026**, you get 2 years under the current rules. Don't sit on it.

**If you finish in 2027:** you'll get 18 months unless your university makes the high-compliance list. Plan your job search for the shorter window. That gives you less time to find a Skilled Worker sponsor.

**If you're choosing a university now:** once the list is published, it becomes a real factor. An extra 6 months of work rights can be the difference between finding a sponsor and going home.

## Quick tips to make the most of your Graduate visa

- **Start job hunting in your final term,** not after graduation.
- **Target employers who already hold a sponsor licence.** The UK government publishes the full register of licensed sponsors.
- **Keep the fee ready.** The Graduate visa application fee plus the Immigration Health Surcharge for the full period adds up to a few thousand pounds.

**Quick gist:** For UK Student visa applications made **on or after 30 November 2026**, the monthly living-cost amount rises to **£1,570 in London** and **£1,203 outside London**. You show up to 9 months, so that's up to £14,130 in London or £10,827 elsewhere, plus any unpaid first-year tuition.

If you're targeting the January 2027 intake, this lands right in the middle of your application season. Here's what you need to know.

## The new amounts

The change comes from the Home Office's Statement of Changes HC 584, laid before Parliament on 3 September 2026.

| | Before 30 Nov 2026 | From 30 Nov 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| London (per month) | £1,529 | £1,570 |
| Outside London (per month) | £1,171 | £1,203 |
| London, 9 months | £13,761 | £14,130 |
| Outside London, 9 months | £10,539 | £10,827 |

The rise is about £300–£370 over 9 months. That's not huge in pounds, but at today's exchange rate it's roughly half a million naira or more extra to keep in your account.

## What doesn't change

- The **28-day rule**: the full amount must sit in your account for 28 consecutive days, ending no more than 31 days before you apply.
- The visa fee and the Immigration Health Surcharge aren't part of this change.
- Applications **submitted before 30 November** are assessed on the old amounts.

## Apply before or after?

If your CAS is ready, your money has already been sitting for 28 days and you can submit before 30 November, you'll be assessed at the lower rate. But don't rush a sloppy application to save a few hundred pounds. A refusal costs far more.

If you're applying after 30 November, top up your balance **before** your 28-day period starts. The amount must be in your account for the whole 28 days. You can't add the extra halfway through.

## Naira savers: watch the exchange rate

If your funds are in naira, the Home Office converts them using the OANDA rate **on the day you apply**, not the day you deposit. If the naira weakens during your 28 days, you could fall short. A 5–10% buffer is a sensible safety margin.

**Quick gist:** Canada released its 2027–2029 Immigration Levels Plan on 5 November 2026. It keeps **380,000 new permanent residents a year**, but the federal Express Entry target falls to **100,000** and provincial programmes rise to **95,000**. There's also a one-off measure for up to 200,000 people already in Canada. On 25 November, MPs voted down an attempt to reject the plan, so it stands.

For Nigerians eyeing Canada, the message is simple: **the provinces matter more than ever.**

## The headline numbers

- **380,000** permanent residents a year from 2027 to 2029.
- **370,000** new temporary residents (workers and students) a year.
- **Express Entry (Federal High Skilled): 100,000** in 2027, down from a planned 111,000.
- **Provincial Nominee Program (PNP): 95,000**, up.
- **Up to 200,000 extra PR spaces in 2027** through a time-limited measure for people already living in Canada, including up to 45,000 spaces for spouses, partners and children of Canadians and permanent residents.

## What it means if you're still in Nigeria

Express Entry is still the main door for skilled workers abroad, but it's getting narrower. Recent draws have favoured people with Canadian work experience, provincial nominations and French skills. A high score from abroad alone is getting harder to rely on.

**Practical moves:**

1. **Look seriously at provincial programmes.** Many provinces run streams for in-demand jobs such as healthcare, trades and tech, and some accept people who are outside Canada.
2. **Consider French.** French-language draws have been among the biggest this year. Even moderate French can add a lot of points.
3. **Max out your English score.** Every point on IELTS or CELPIP counts.

## What's happening with Express Entry draws?

On 3 November 2026, new Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) rules took effect, including a new "labour market integration" factor and the return of points for job offers without an LMIA. IRCC paused invitation rounds while it recalculated every profile in the pool. The last round before the pause was on 28 October. Check IRCC's rounds page for the latest draw, because cut-off scores may move once rounds settle under the new rules.

## Already in Canada on a work or study permit?

The 200,000 extra spaces for 2027 are aimed at you. Details are being released group by group. IRCC published the breakdown for spouses and dependants on 10–12 November. Keep your documents current and watch for the stream that fits you.

## Bottom line

Canada isn't closing its doors, but it's choosing more carefully. If you're serious, build a plan B around a specific province.

**Quick gist:** The UK has revoked the Global Talent visas of two Nigerian men after a BBC investigation found they had used fabricated press reviews to pose as fine-art photographers. The deception came to light when the art critic Molara Wood found reviews published under her name that she never wrote.

This story spread fast in Nigerian WhatsApp groups. Beyond the gossip, there's a serious lesson in it for anyone planning to japa.

## What happened

The Global Talent visa is meant for leaders and rising stars in fields such as arts, science and tech. You don't need a job offer, but you do need a strong body of evidence: recognition, reviews, awards and endorsements.

According to reports by TheCable and Legit.ng, the two men presented themselves as photographers with a track record of reviewed work. Molara Wood, an art critic, discovered articles attributed to her that she had never written. She also described the "reviews" and the artworks as poor quality. A BBC investigation followed, and the Home Office moved to revoke the visas.

## Why fake documents always come back to bite

Getting the visa isn't the end of the checking. The Home Office can revoke a visa **at any point** if it finds it was obtained by deception. Once that happens:

- You can be **removed** from the UK.
- You can face a **ban on re-entry** for up to 10 years.
- Future applications to the UK, and often to Canada, the US and Australia, will ask whether you've ever been refused or used deception. Lying on those forms makes it worse.
- Under the new settlement plans, any immigration breach adds years to your path to ILR.

And the people who sold you the "package" are nowhere to be found when it all falls apart.

## How to spot a dodgy japa agent

- **They guarantee a visa.** Nobody can.
- **They offer to "arrange" documents** such as bank statements, job letters, reviews, references or certificates.
- **They ask you to pay big sums in cash** or to personal accounts.
- **They won't put anything in writing.**
- **In the UK, they aren't registered.** Anyone giving UK immigration advice must be regulated by the IAA (formerly OISC) or be a solicitor. You can check the official register online.

## The honest route is slower but safer

If Global Talent is your dream, build real evidence. Exhibit, publish, enter competitions and get genuine endorsements. It may take a year or two longer, but a visa you earned can't be taken from you.
