**Quick gist:** Germany's Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte), introduced in June 2024, lets skilled people from outside the EU move to Germany for up to a year to look for work. You qualify either with a fully recognised foreign qualification or by scoring enough points for things like qualifications, language skills, experience, age and links to Germany. You must be able to support yourself while you search.

## Why Germany is on the japa map

Germany has labour shortages in many sectors, including healthcare, engineering, IT and skilled trades. It has been reforming its immigration rules to attract workers from outside the EU.

## Two ways to qualify

**1. Full recognition:** if your vocational or university qualification is fully recognised in Germany, you can qualify directly.

**2. Points system:** if not, you need:

- A foreign qualification recognised by the state where it was obtained (for example, a Nigerian degree)
- Basic German (A1) or good English (B2)
- Enough points, awarded for factors such as partial recognition of qualifications, work experience, German or English skills, age and previous time in Germany

## Proof of funds

You must show you can support yourself during the job search, usually through a blocked account or a formal declaration of commitment. Check the current monthly amount required.

## What you can do on it

- Look for skilled work
- Do part-time work while searching, within limits
- Take trial jobs

Once you find a qualifying job, you can switch to a work permit or EU Blue Card.

## Language matters

Even if you qualify with English, learning German greatly improves your job prospects and everyday life. Many employers expect at least some German.

## Is it right for you?

It may suit:

- Skilled professionals in shortage fields
- People willing to learn German
- Those with savings to support a job search

## The bottom line

The Opportunity Card opens a new door to Europe for skilled Nigerians. Check your points, start learning German and prepare your finances before applying.

*This is general information, not immigration advice. Check official German government sources.*

---
**Quick gist:** Ireland's Critical Skills Employment Permit is designed to attract highly skilled workers in occupations where there are shortages, such as ICT, engineering, healthcare and some finance roles. You need a job offer that meets salary and occupation requirements. It can lead to long-term residence and lets family join more easily than some other permits.

## Why consider Ireland

Ireland is English-speaking, has a strong tech and pharmaceutical sector, and is part of the EU. A growing Nigerian community lives there.

## The basics

You'll usually need:

- A job offer for at least two years
- An occupation on the Critical Skills Occupations List, with a salary meeting the threshold, or a higher salary for other eligible roles
- Relevant qualifications or experience

Salary thresholds are set by the government and change over time, so check the latest figures.

## Family

Holders of a Critical Skills permit can usually bring their spouse or partner and children. Spouses and partners have had improved access to work in recent years.

## Path to residence

After a period on the permit, holders may be able to apply for a Stamp 4 permission, which allows them to live and work in Ireland without needing an employment permit.

## Costs and process

The employer or employee applies online. There's a fee, and processing times vary. Once the permit is granted, a visa may be required to enter Ireland, followed by registration with immigration authorities.

## Things to consider

- Housing in Dublin is expensive and hard to find.
- The cost of living is high.
- Healthcare works differently from the NHS.

## The bottom line

For skilled professionals, Ireland's Critical Skills permit can be an attractive route to Europe. Research the occupation list, salary rules and living costs before you commit.

*This is general information, not immigration advice.*

photo: Nigeria flag waving
---
**Quick gist:** In May 2024, President Bola Tinubu signed a law restoring "Nigeria, We Hail Thee" as Nigeria's national anthem. It was the anthem adopted at independence in 1960 and had been replaced in 1978 by "Arise, O Compatriots." Supporters see it as reconnecting with history; critics questioned the timing and the anthem's colonial-era origins.

## A tale of two anthems

- **"Nigeria, We Hail Thee" (1960–1978):** the independence anthem. Its words were written by Lillian Jean Williams, a British expatriate, and its music by Frances Berda.
- **"Arise, O Compatriots" (1978–2024):** adopted under the military government, with words drawn from entries by Nigerians and music arranged by the Nigerian Police Band.

## The 2024 change

The National Assembly passed a bill restoring the old anthem, and the President signed it into law in May 2024. It was first sung officially at events marking Democracy Day.

## Why supporters backed it

- It reflects Nigeria's independence and early national identity.
- Some argued its lyrics emphasise unity across diversity.

## Why critics objected

- The process was quick, with limited public consultation.
- Some felt there were more urgent priorities given economic hardship.
- Others noted the lyrics were written by a non-Nigerian.

## Teaching children abroad

Many diaspora parents who grew up singing "Arise, O Compatriots" now teach their children the new (old) anthem. It's a good conversation starter about Nigeria's history.

## The pledge

The National Pledge, recited in schools, remains in use.

## The bottom line

The anthem change sparked debate at home and abroad. Whatever your view, it's a reminder that national symbols carry history and meaning.

Ask for a receipt or confirmation for every payment. Keep them for your records.

Naira fees can feel unpredictable when converting from pounds. Some families send money in instalments or set aside a fees fund in advance.

Stay in touch with the school and the child's guardian about progress, not just payments.

## The bottom line

Pay schools directly, verify account details, keep receipts and plan for currency changes.

**Quick gist:** Many people who move abroad experience stress, homesickness, loneliness or low mood, especially in the first year. It's common and nothing to be ashamed of. Staying connected, keeping routines, being active and asking for help early all make a difference. In England, you can refer yourself to free NHS Talking Therapies.

## Why moving is hard on the mind

- Separation from family and friends
- Money pressure, including supporting family back home
- Visa and job uncertainty
- Cold, dark winters
- Culture shock and discrimination

## Signs to watch for

- Feeling low or hopeless for weeks
- Trouble sleeping or sleeping too much
- Losing interest in things you enjoyed
- Withdrawing from people
- Feeling constantly anxious or on edge

## Things that help

- **Stay connected:** call family, but also build local friendships.
- **Routine:** regular sleep, meals and activity.
- **Move your body:** walks, parkrun, gym or football.
- **Daylight:** get outside during daylight hours in winter.
- **Limit doom-scrolling:** especially news that adds stress.
- **Faith and community:** many people find strength in church, mosque or community groups.

## Getting professional help

- **GP:** your first point of contact.
- **NHS Talking Therapies:** free talking therapy in England; you can often refer yourself online.
- **Charities:** many offer helplines and support.
- **Urgent help:** if you feel unsafe, call NHS 111 (option for mental health) or 999 in an emergency.

## Breaking the stigma

In some communities, mental health is rarely discussed. But asking for help is a sign of strength, not weakness.

## The bottom line

Your mental health matters as much as your job and your visa. Look after yourself, and reach out early if you're struggling.
