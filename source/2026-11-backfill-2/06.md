---
**Quick gist:** Nigerian parents usually need a Standard Visitor visa to visit the UK. The decision rests on whether they're genuinely visiting, can afford the trip and will return home. Strong evidence of ties to Nigeria and a clear, honest invitation from you make a big difference.

## What the visa allows

A Standard Visitor visa lets people visit for tourism, to see family, or for certain other activities, usually for up to six months per visit. It doesn't allow work or long stays that look like living in the UK.

Longer-validity visas (2, 5 or 10 years) are available, allowing multiple visits, but each visit is still limited.

## What decision-makers look for

The Home Office considers whether the applicant:

- Is a genuine visitor who will leave at the end of the visit
- Has enough money to cover the trip, or is properly supported by you
- Has strong ties to Nigeria, such as a home, family, business, pension or community roles

## Documents that help

From your parents:

- Valid passport and previous travel history
- Bank statements showing a regular, explainable pattern of income and savings
- Evidence of property, business, pension or employment in Nigeria
- Evidence of family ties at home

From you, if you're sponsoring the trip:

- An invitation letter explaining who you are, the purpose and dates of the visit, and who will pay
- Your passport and UK immigration status (eVisa share code or BRP copy)
- Your payslips, bank statements and employment letter
- Proof of accommodation, like your tenancy agreement or mortgage statement

## Common reasons for refusal

- **Unexplained large deposits** in bank accounts shortly before applying
- **Weak ties** to Nigeria
- **Inconsistencies** between the forms, the invitation letter and the documents
- **Previous immigration problems** that weren't declared

Always tell the truth on the form. Deception can lead to a ban.

## Practical tips

- Apply well ahead of the planned travel date, especially before summer and December.
- Keep the purpose simple and clear.
- If your parents need medical treatment in the UK, that's a different matter, as NHS treatment is generally chargeable for visitors. Arrange travel insurance.

## If they're refused

Read the refusal letter carefully. It explains the reasons. Address each point with better evidence before reapplying, rather than submitting the same application again.

## The bottom line

Help your parents show they're genuine visitors with strong reasons to return home. Clear, consistent, honest evidence is what works.

**Quick gist:** A DBS (Disclosure and Barring Service) check is a criminal record check used by employers in England and Wales. Jobs in care, healthcare, education and work with children or vulnerable adults usually need a standard or enhanced check. Your employer normally starts the process. You may also need a police certificate from Nigeria for time spent there.

## The types of DBS check

- **Basic:** shows unspent convictions. Anyone can apply for their own.
- **Standard:** shows spent and unspent convictions, cautions and warnings, with some filtered out. Used for certain jobs.
- **Enhanced:** includes the standard check plus relevant information from local police.
- **Enhanced with barred list check:** also checks whether you're barred from working with children or adults. Common in care and teaching.

## How to get one

For standard and enhanced checks, your employer or an umbrella body applies on your behalf. You'll fill in an online form and prove your identity with documents like your passport, eVisa, proof of address and bank statements.

For a basic check, you can apply yourself online on GOV.UK.

## Who pays

Often the employer pays, but some employers, especially agencies, ask you to pay. Check before you start. Volunteers can get some checks free.

## The DBS Update Service

If you join the Update Service, your certificate can be kept up to date and checked online by future employers, so you may not need a new check every time you change jobs. There's a yearly fee for paid roles.

## Overseas checks

A DBS check only covers UK records. Employers may also ask for a criminal record certificate from countries where you've lived, such as a Nigerian police certificate. Requirements vary, so ask your employer what they accept. Getting a Nigerian certificate can take time, so start early.

## How long it takes

Many checks come back within a few weeks, but delays can happen, particularly if your address history is complex or you've lived abroad.

## Tips

- Give your full address history accurately.
- Use your name exactly as it appears on your passport.
- Keep your certificate safe; you may need to show the original.

## The bottom line

If you're heading into care, health or education work, expect a DBS check. Have your ID documents ready and ask early about overseas police certificates.

**Quick gist:** Most employees in the UK pay tax through PAYE and don't need to file a tax return. You may need to file a Self Assessment return if you have untaxed income such as self-employment, rental income (including from property in Nigeria) or other foreign income. The deadline for online returns is 31 January after the end of the tax year, and late filing brings penalties.

## Who usually needs to file

You may need to file if you:

- Are self-employed and earned more than the trading allowance
- Received rental income
- Had untaxed foreign income
- Earned above certain thresholds or had complex income
- Need to pay the High Income Child Benefit Charge (rules on this have been changing)

Use the GOV.UK checker to confirm your situation.

## Foreign income: the big one for diaspora

If you're UK resident, you may need to report income from abroad, such as rent from a flat in Lagos or interest from a Nigerian account. The rules on foreign income for UK residents changed significantly from April 2025, when the old remittance basis for non-domiciled people was replaced. Get advice if this affects you.

The UK and Nigeria have a tax treaty designed to prevent double taxation, so tax paid in Nigeria may be credited against UK tax.

## Key dates

The UK tax year runs from 6 April to 5 April. For most people:

- **5 October:** register for Self Assessment if it's your first time
- **31 January:** deadline for online returns and paying any tax owed

## How to file

1. Register with HMRC and get your Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR).
2. Keep records of income and expenses.
3. File online through your HMRC account.
4. Pay what you owe by the deadline.

## Penalties

Missing the deadline usually means an automatic £100 penalty, with more added the longer it's late. Interest is charged on late payments.

## Should you get an accountant?

If you have rental income abroad, a business or complicated circumstances, an accountant can save you time and money. Choose one who's a member of a recognised professional body.

## Beware of HMRC scams

HMRC won't threaten you with arrest by phone or ask for payment in gift cards. If in doubt, log in to your HMRC account directly.

## The bottom line

If you only earn a UK salary, you probably don't need to file. If you have income from Nigeria or side work, check whether you need to register before the deadline.

Use only official channels.

## Why you might need it

- Applying for or renewing a Nigerian passport
- Visa or immigration applications that ask for proof of birth
- Registering your own children
- Correcting errors in other documents

## Types of documents

- **Birth certificate:** issued by the NPC for registered births.
- **Attestation of birth:** for people whose births weren't registered at the time, often used by adults.
- **Declaration of age:** a sworn declaration sometimes accepted when no birth record exists, though many institutions now prefer NPC documents.

## How Nigerians abroad usually apply

1. **Start online** through the NPC's official portal where available, filling in your details.
2. **Provide supporting documents** such as your passport, parents' details and any existing records.
3. **Use a trusted representative** in Nigeria if an in-person step is needed, or check whether your embassy can help.
4. **Pay official fees** only through approved channels.
5. **Collect or receive** the certificate and get it legalised if a foreign authority needs it.

## Legalisation for use abroad

If a UK or other foreign authority needs your Nigerian document, it may need to be legalised or authenticated by Nigeria's Ministry of Foreign Affairs and sometimes the foreign embassy. Ask the organisation requesting it exactly what they need.

- **Fake certificates:** some agents sell fake documents. Using them can ruin immigration applications and lead to bans.
- **Name mismatches:** make sure your names and date of birth match your passport and NIN.
- **Unofficial websites:** check that you're on the real NPC site.

## Registering your child born abroad

If your child was born in the UK, their birth is registered in the UK. To get them a Nigerian passport, you'll typically use their UK birth certificate plus evidence of your Nigerian citizenship.

## The bottom line

Use official NPC channels, keep your details consistent across documents and start early if you need certificates for a passport or visa.

## Christmas Day is quiet

On 25 December in England, large shops are not allowed to open, and most public transport stops. Boxing Day (26 December) is also a bank holiday, though many shops reopen for sales. Plan travel and shopping in advance.

## Food and shopping

Supermarkets get very busy in the week before Christmas. Buy non-perishables early and order online if you can. African shops also stock up on Christmas favourites, but popular items can sell out, so don't leave it late.

## Church and community

Many African churches hold carol services, Christmas Eve services and Christmas Day services. They're a great way to feel part of a community. Some community associations host parties between Christmas and New Year.

## Work and holidays

If you work in care, health or hospitality, you may be on shift. Check your rota and pay rules early. Bank holiday pay depends on your contract.

- Cook a proper Nigerian Christmas feast: jollof, fried rice, chicken, small chops.

- Invite other new arrivals who are alone.
- Walk in the park or watch a Christmas film.

## The money side

Christmas can be expensive. Set a budget for gifts, food and money home. Watch out for buy-now-pay-later traps in December.

It's common to feel homesick around Christmas, especially the first year. Reach out to friends, church or community groups. Volunteering at a Christmas lunch for people on their own is a lovely way to spend the day and meet people.

Your first UK Christmas might feel quiet, but with a little planning it can be warm and memorable. Make room for old traditions and new ones.

- ***Things Fall Apart* by Chinua Achebe (1958):** the story of Okonkwo and the arrival of colonialism in an Igbo village. Short, powerful and essential.

- **Ben Okri's *The Famished Road*:** won the Booker Prize in 1991.
- **Oyinkan Braithwaite's *My Sister, the Serial Killer*:** a darkly funny Lagos thriller.

## For teenagers

- ***Children of Blood and Bone* by Tomi Adeyemi:** fantasy inspired by Yoruba culture and mythology.
- **Nnedi Okorafor's books:** science fiction and fantasy rooted in Nigerian culture.

## For younger children

Look for picture books by Nigerian and Nigerian-British authors that feature Nigerian names, food and families. Many libraries and bookshops now stock these.

## Make reading a family habit

- Read a chapter together before bed.
- Talk about the history behind the story.

Nigerian literature helps children abroad understand where they come from, and it's simply brilliant writing. Start with one book and see where it leads.

## Business name vs company

- **Business name:** simpler and cheaper, suitable for sole traders and small partnerships. The owner is personally responsible for debts.
- **Private limited company (Ltd):** a separate legal entity, better for growth, investment and limiting personal liability.

## What you'll typically need

- A proposed business name (and backups)
- Details of directors, shareholders or proprietors, including valid ID
- A business address in Nigeria
- Description of business activities
- Share capital details for a company

## The process

1. **Check name availability** on the CAC portal.
2. **Reserve the name.**
3. **Complete registration forms** online with the details above.
4. **Pay the fees** through official channels.
5. **Get your certificate** and related documents.

Many diaspora founders use accredited CAC agents or lawyers to handle the details. Check that your agent is accredited and get receipts for everything.

## After registration

- **Tax:** register for a Tax Identification Number and understand your tax obligations.
- **Bank account:** open a corporate account with your CAC documents.
- **Annual returns:** companies must file annual returns with CAC to stay in good standing.

## Running a business from abroad

- **Trusted people on the ground:** choose managers carefully and use written agreements.
- **Separate finances:** never mix personal and business money.
- **Regular reporting:** ask for weekly or monthly reports and visit when you can.
- **Start small:** test the idea before investing heavily.

## Also think about your UK side

If you're in the UK on a visa, check whether running a business abroad affects your visa conditions, and report income to HMRC if required.

## The bottom line

Registering a Nigerian business from abroad is easier than ever, but success depends on trusted people, good records and realistic plans.

**Quick gist:** The Life in the UK test is a computer-based test about British history, traditions and everyday life. You usually need to pass it to apply for settlement (Indefinite Leave to Remain) and British citizenship. It has 24 questions, and you need 18 correct answers to pass. Book only through the official GOV.UK service.

## Who needs to take it

Most people aged 18 to 64 applying for ILR or citizenship need to pass the test, unless exempt. If you passed it for your ILR application, you don't need to take it again for citizenship.

## What the test covers

Questions come from the official handbook, *Life in the United Kingdom: A Guide for New Residents*. Topics include:

- British history, from early Britain to modern times
- The UK's government, law and institutions
- Traditions, culture, sport and famous people
- Everyday life and responsibilities

Some questions are surprisingly detailed, so don't rely on general knowledge.

## How to prepare

- Read the official handbook carefully, more than once.
- Use practice tests to get used to the question style.
- Focus on dates, names and numbers.
- Study in short daily sessions over a few weeks.

## Booking and taking the test

Book online through GOV.UK only; unofficial sites charge extra. You'll need ID that matches your booking exactly. The test takes place at approved centres and lasts 45 minutes.

## After you pass

You'll get a unique reference number to use in your application. Keep it safe.

## Citizenship: the other requirements

To naturalise, you'll usually need:

- To have held ILR or settled status for the required time (or be married to a British citizen)
- To meet residence requirements, including limits on time spent outside the UK
- To be of good character
- To meet the English language requirement
- To pass the Life in the UK test

The fee is significant, so make sure you qualify before applying. After approval, you attend a citizenship ceremony.

## Dual nationality

The UK allows dual citizenship, and Nigerians by birth generally keep Nigerian citizenship too.

## The bottom line

The Life in the UK test is very passable with proper study. Use the official handbook, practise and book through GOV.UK.
