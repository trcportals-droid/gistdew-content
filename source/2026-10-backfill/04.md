**Quick gist:** Before you pay for land in Nigeria, verify who really owns it, what title documents exist and whether the land is free from government acquisition or disputes. Use an independent lawyer and surveyor you choose yourself, search the land registry, and never rely only on a relative or agent's word.

## Why diaspora buyers are targets

Fraudsters know Nigerians abroad can't easily visit the site. Common scams include selling the same plot to several buyers, selling land under government acquisition, and fake documents. Even honest family members can be misled.

## Key documents to understand

- **Certificate of Occupancy (C of O):** issued by a state government, showing a right of occupancy over the land.
- **Governor's Consent:** needed when land with a C of O changes hands. Buying without it leaves your title weak.
- **Deed of Assignment:** the agreement transferring the seller's interest to you. It should be properly stamped and registered.
- **Survey plan:** shows the exact location and boundaries, prepared by a registered surveyor.
- **Excision or Gazette:** in some states, community land must be excised from government acquisition before it can be sold.

Requirements differ by state, so your lawyer should explain what applies to your plot.

## Checks to make

1. **Land registry search.** Your lawyer can search the state land registry to confirm the title and whether it's encumbered.
2. **Survey verification.** Have the survey plan checked at the Surveyor-General's office to confirm the land isn't under acquisition.
3. **Physical inspection.** Someone you trust, ideally independent, should visit, take dated photos and talk to neighbours.
4. **Family land.** If buying from a family, make sure the right family representatives sign and that there's a family resolution.

## Paying safely

- Pay into a traceable account in the seller's or company's name, never cash
- Get receipts and the signed agreement
- Pay in stages tied to documents where possible

## Estates and developers

Buying in an estate can be easier, but check the developer's title, track record and whether infrastructure promises are realistic. Read the contract carefully.

## After you buy

Register your deed, keep copies of everything safely (including scans abroad) and visit or have someone check the land regularly. Fencing and building help show possession.

## The bottom line

Land can be a great investment, but only if your title is solid. Spend on proper legal and survey checks; they cost far less than losing your money.

## Step 1: an emergency fund

Life abroad comes with surprise costs: a broken boiler, a visa fee, a flight home for a family emergency. An emergency fund stops these turning into debt. Aim for three to six months of essential spending, saved in an easy-access account.

## Step 2: understand ISAs

An Individual Savings Account (ISA) lets you save or invest without paying tax on the interest or growth. Each tax year there's an overall ISA allowance you can split between types:

- **Cash ISA:** like a savings account, but the interest is tax-free
- **Stocks and Shares ISA:** for investing in funds or shares; can rise and fall in value

If you're employed and eligible, you're automatically enrolled. You pay in, your employer pays in and you get tax relief. Staying in is usually one of the best returns you can get, because the employer contribution is effectively free money.

If you might leave the UK one day, your pension doesn't disappear. You can usually access it from abroad when you reach pension age, though transferring it overseas has complex rules and possible tax charges. Get regulated advice before doing that.

## Step 4: investing for the long term

If you're investing, think long term (five years or more), spread your money across many companies with low-cost funds and don't put in money you'll need soon.

## Red flags

- Promises of guaranteed high returns
- Pressure to invest quickly
- "Forex trading" or crypto schemes promoted by friends or influencers
- Companies not authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority

Check the FCA register before handing over any money.

## The bottom line

Build your safety net, use tax-free allowances, keep your pension and stay away from schemes that sound too good to be true.

Canada has been limiting the number of international student permits, so check the latest rules early.

## Step 1: choose a designated learning institution

Only schools on IRCC's DLI list can enrol international students on a study permit. Check the list before applying, and research whether your programme makes you eligible for a post-graduation work permit if that matters to you.

## Step 2: the provincial attestation letter

In recent years, Canada introduced a cap on study permits, and most applicants now need a provincial or territorial attestation letter (PAL or TAL) from the province where they'll study. Your school usually helps you get it. Some groups are exempt, so check IRCC guidance.

You must show you can pay your first year's tuition plus living costs. The living cost amount was raised significantly in recent years and is updated over time, so check IRCC's current figure. Apply online with your documents, pay the fees and give your biometrics. Processing times vary, so apply as early as possible.

Most full-time students with a valid study permit can work off campus up to a set number of hours per week during academic sessions and full-time during scheduled breaks. The weekly limit has changed in recent years, so check the current rule.
