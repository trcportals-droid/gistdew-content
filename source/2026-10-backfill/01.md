**Quick gist:** You can renew a Nigerian passport without travelling home. You apply and pay online through the Nigeria Immigration Service (NIS) portal, then attend a biometric appointment at the High Commission in London or another approved centre. Start early: processing can take weeks, and you need a valid passport for many UK immigration steps.

## Why you shouldn't leave it late

In the UK, your Nigerian passport is more than a travel document. Your eVisa is linked to it, employers and landlords may check it, and you'll need it to travel home for weddings, burials and Detty December. If it expires, you'll have to update your passport details on your UKVI account once you get the new one. Renewing six to nine months before expiry gives you breathing room.

## What you'll usually need

Requirements change, so always confirm on the NIS and High Commission websites before you apply. Typically you'll need:

- Your current or expired Nigerian passport (the data page and the page with your UK visa or residence details)
- Your National Identification Number (NIN). The NIN is now central to passport applications, so if you don't have one, sort that out first
- A recent passport photo that meets the published guidelines
- Proof of your UK status, such as your eVisa share code or residence documents
- Payment confirmation from the online portal

If your name has changed through marriage or deed poll, bring the supporting documents, such as a marriage certificate or a sworn affidavit and newspaper publication. Name mismatches are one of the biggest causes of delay.

## The process in five steps

1. **Apply online.** Go to the official NIS portal, choose passport renewal or reissue, pick the passport type (32 or 64 pages, 5 or 10 years validity where available) and fill in the form carefully.
2. **Pay the fee online.** Only pay on the official portal. Keep your payment receipt and application reference.
3. **Book your appointment.** Choose the London High Commission or another approved capture centre. Slots can fill up fast, especially before December.
4. **Attend your biometric capture.** Bring originals and copies of everything. Your fingerprints and photo are taken here.
5. **Collect your passport.** You'll be told when it's ready, either for collection in person or by an approved delivery option.

## Mistakes that slow people down

- **Using agents or "connect" people.** Anyone promising a passport in two days for extra money is a risk to your documents and your data. Use the official route.
- **Different spellings.** If your name or date of birth on your NIN, old passport and birth certificate don't match exactly, fix it before you apply.
- **Wrong photo.** Shadows, glasses and coloured backgrounds can get your photo rejected.
- **Paying on fake websites.** Scam sites copy the look of government portals. Check the web address carefully.

## After you get your new passport

Update your UKVI account with the new passport details so your eVisa stays linked to the right document. If you have a bank or employer record that uses your passport number, update that too.

## The bottom line

Renewing from abroad is very doable if you start early, use only official channels and make sure your names match across every document.

Three AFCON titles, an Olympic gold and many unforgettable nights. Whatever happens in the next tournament, the history explains why every Super Eagles match feels like a family event.

You can start work before it arrives.

## What it is and why it matters

Your NI number is a personal reference that stays the same for life. Your employer uses it to report your earnings and deductions to HMRC. It also counts towards your record for the UK State Pension and some benefits, so you want your contributions recorded correctly from your very first payslip.

## Check if you already have one

Before applying, check your documents. Many people who received a biometric residence permit (BRP) had their NI number printed on the back. If you're on an eVisa, look through the letters and emails you received about your visa. If it's there, you don't need to apply.

## How to apply

If you don't have one, apply on the official GOV.UK page. It's free, so ignore any website asking you to pay for an NI number.

You'll usually need:

- The right to work in the UK
- Your passport and visa details
- A UK address

You may be asked to prove your identity, and HMRC will contact you if it needs more information. Your number will then arrive by post.

## Can I start work without it?

Yes. You can start a job as long as you can prove your right to work. Tell your employer you've applied and give them the number when it arrives. Until then, your employer can still pay you.

## Common problems

- **Emergency tax code.** If your first payslip shows a code like 1257L W1 or M1, you may be on an emergency tax code. It usually sorts itself out, but if you've overpaid, HMRC will refund you or adjust your code. Check your Personal Tax Account on GOV.UK.
- **Wrong name format.** Make sure the name your employer has matches your passport exactly.
- **Lost number.** You can find it in your Personal Tax Account or on old payslips and P60s.

## Protect it

Your NI number is personal information. Only share it with your employer, HMRC, your pension provider and other legitimate organisations. Scammers sometimes call pretending to be HMRC and threaten arrest over "unpaid National Insurance". HMRC does not do that.

## The bottom line

Check your visa documents first. If you need a number, apply free on GOV.UK, start work in the meantime and keep an eye on your tax code.

**Quick gist:** Anyone in England can register with a GP surgery, and you don't need proof of address, ID or immigration status to register. ## What a GP does

A GP (general practitioner) is your family doctor. They're your first stop for most health problems that aren't emergencies, and they refer you to hospital specialists when you need them. Without a GP, getting repeat prescriptions, referrals and vaccinations is much harder.

## How to register

1. **Find surgeries near you.** Use the NHS "find a GP" service and enter your postcode. Surgeries only accept patients who live in their catchment area.
2. **Register online or in person.** Many surgeries let you register online through the NHS website or the NHS App. Others give you a paper form.
3. **Fill in your details.** You'll be asked for your name, date of birth, address and NHS number if you have one. Don't worry if you don't; it will be created for you.
4. **Health check.** Some surgeries invite new patients for a short health check with a nurse.

## Get the NHS App

Once you're registered, download the NHS App. You can book appointments, order repeat prescriptions and see parts of your record. Logging in requires identity checks, so have your passport ready.

## What if it's urgent?

- **Emergency (chest pain, severe bleeding, difficulty breathing):** call 999 or go to A&E.
- **Urgent but not life-threatening:** call 111 or use NHS 111 online. They'll tell you where to go.
- **Minor illness:** your local pharmacist can help with many common problems without an appointment.

## Prescriptions in England

In England, most adults pay a fixed charge for each prescribed item, though some groups get them free. If you need regular medicine, ask about a prescription prepayment certificate, which can save money.

## Tips from people who've been through it

- Register even if you feel fine. Getting an appointment is faster when you're already on the list.
- Bring a list of any medicines you take and your vaccination history.
- If English isn't your first language, you can ask for an interpreter. It's free.
- Book early in the morning when lines open; that's when appointments go fastest.

## The bottom line

Registering with a GP is free, straightforward and one of the best things you can do in your first month in the UK.

**Quick gist:** The naira-to-pound rate moves mainly because of supply and demand for foreign currency in Nigeria. Oil earnings, foreign investment, inflation, interest rates and Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) policy all play a part. For people sending money home, the practical lesson is simple: compare rates and fees every time, and don't try to time the market perfectly.

## Supply and demand for dollars and pounds

Nigerians need foreign currency to import goods, pay school fees abroad and travel. Foreign currency comes into Nigeria from oil exports, foreign investors, remittances from the diaspora and other exports. When more people want foreign currency than is available, the naira weakens. When more comes in, it can strengthen.

## Oil still matters

Oil is a huge source of Nigeria's foreign earnings. When oil prices are high and production is steady, more dollars flow into the economy. When production falls or prices drop, there are fewer dollars to go round, and the naira usually feels the pressure.

## Inflation

When prices in Nigeria rise faster than prices in the UK, each naira buys less over time. Over the long run, high inflation tends to weaken a currency against those of countries with lower inflation.

## Interest rates and investors

Foreign investors move money into a country when they can earn good returns and get their money out easily. When the CBN raises interest rates or makes it easier to repatriate funds, that can attract investment and support the naira. When investors worry about getting their money out, they stay away.

## Policy changes

In recent years, Nigeria has made big changes to how its exchange rate works, moving away from multiple official rates towards a more market-based system. These shifts caused big swings, and the gap between official and street rates has narrowed and widened at different times. The CBN's announcements can move the rate quickly, so it's worth following reliable news.

## What this means when you send money home

- **Compare the total cost.** A provider might advertise "zero fees" but give you a poor exchange rate. Compare how many naira actually arrive for every £100.
- **Check the mid-market rate.** Search for the current GBP to NGN rate and see how close each provider gets to it.
- **Don't chase the perfect day.** Nobody predicts currency moves reliably. If your family needs money for rent or school fees, send it when it's needed.
- **Use regulated providers.** In the UK, check that the company is authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority.
- **Be careful with informal channels.** They can look cheaper but carry real risks, including losing your money with no way to recover it.

## The bottom line

The naira's value reflects Nigeria's oil earnings, inflation, interest rates and policy choices. You can't control any of that, but you can control which provider you use and how much you pay in hidden costs.

**Quick gist:** "Afrobeat" (no s) is the genre Fela Anikulapo-Kuti pioneered in the 1970s: long, horn-heavy, politically charged songs built on jazz, funk and Yoruba rhythms. "Afrobeats" (with an s) is the umbrella term for the modern pop sound from Nigeria and Ghana that now fills arenas around the world. They're related, but they're not the same thing.

## Afrobeat: Fela's revolution

In the late 1960s and 1970s, Fela blended highlife, jazz, funk and traditional Yoruba music into something new. His songs could run for 10 or 20 minutes, with big horn sections, interlocking guitars and drummer Tony Allen's famous grooves. Fela's lyrics attacked corruption and military rule, and his Kalakuta Republic compound in Lagos became a symbol of resistance.

Afrobeat is still alive today. Femi Kuti and Seun Kuti carry on their father's sound, and bands around the world play it.

## Afrobeats: the global pop wave

Afrobeats grew in the 2000s and 2010s, mixing West African pop, hip-hop, dancehall, R&B and house. Songs are shorter and built for radio, clubs and social media. Artists from Nigeria and Ghana, with huge support from the diaspora in London and beyond, took the sound global.

The term "Afrobeats" was popularised partly by DJs and promoters in the UK, who used it to describe the new wave of African pop on their playlists and radio shows.

## Where the two meet

Many Afrobeats stars openly credit Fela as an influence. You'll hear echoes of his horns, call-and-response vocals and attitude in modern songs. Some artists sample him directly. So while the genres are different, Afrobeats definitely grew up listening to Afrobeat.

## Other sounds you'll hear about

- **Highlife:** the guitar-led sound from Ghana and Nigeria that came before both
- **Amapiano:** the South African house style that has hugely influenced recent Afrobeats hits
- **Afro-fusion:** a term some artists use to describe music that blends Afrobeats with other genres
- **Alté:** an alternative, experimental scene from Lagos

## Why the diaspora matters

London, Toronto, Houston and other cities with big Nigerian communities helped push Afrobeats onto global charts. Diaspora DJs, radio hosts and fans built the early audience that later grew into sold-out arena shows.

## The bottom line

If someone calls a modern chart hit "Afrobeat", you can gently correct them: it's Afrobeats, with an s. And if they've never listened to Fela, send them a few of his classics. They'll understand where it all started.

## The basics

To qualify, you generally need:

- A job offer from a UK employer approved by the Home Office as a sponsor
- A job that's on the list of eligible occupations
- A salary that meets the general threshold or the "going rate" for your job, whichever is higher, unless you qualify for a lower rate
- English at the required level
- A Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS) reference number from your employer

## Check the sponsor is real

The Home Office publishes a register of licensed sponsors. Before you trust any job offer, search for the company on that list. If it isn't there, it can't sponsor you. This one check can save you from many scams.

- The visa application fee, which depends on how long your visa is for and whether you apply from outside or inside the UK

Whether you can bring your partner and children depends on your occupation and the rules in force when you apply. The rules for care workers in particular have changed in recent years, so check carefully.

- The job doesn't match the occupation code on the CoS
- The salary doesn't meet the threshold for that occupation
- Missing or inconsistent documents
- A previous immigration history that wasn't declared

If you change employer, you usually need a new CoS and a new application before you start the new job. You can't just switch.

Time on a Skilled Worker visa can count towards Indefinite Leave to Remain. The qualifying period and requirements have been changing, so check the latest settlement rules if long-term residence is your goal.

## The bottom line

A genuine sponsor, a genuine job and a careful application are everything. Check the sponsor register, never pay for a CoS, and read GOV.UK yourself.

**Quick gist:** Your UK payslip shows what you earned (gross pay), what was taken off (income tax, National Insurance, pension and anything else) and what you actually receive (net pay). The most important thing to check is your tax code, because a wrong one can mean paying too much tax for months.

## Gross pay

This is your pay before deductions. For hourly workers, check that the hours match what you actually worked. For salaried workers, it's usually your annual salary divided by 12.

## Tax code

Your tax code tells your employer how much you can earn before paying income tax. A common code for someone with one job and no extra benefits is 1257L, though thresholds can change in future budgets.

Watch out for:

- **Emergency codes** ending in W1, M1 or X. These are common in a first job and often get corrected.
- **BR** (everything taxed at the basic rate) or **0T** (no tax-free allowance). If you only have one job, these could mean you're overpaying.

You can check and correct your tax code in your Personal Tax Account on GOV.UK.

## Income tax

This is the tax deducted through PAYE (Pay As You Earn). Your employer sends it to HMRC for you. If you only have a job and no other income, you usually don't need to file a tax return.

## National Insurance

NI is deducted when you earn above a certain level. It builds your record for the State Pension and some benefits. Your NI number should be on your payslip; if it's missing, give it to HR.

## Pension

Most employers must automatically enrol eligible workers into a workplace pension. You contribute a percentage of your pay, your employer adds a contribution, and you get tax relief. You can opt out, but you'd lose your employer's contribution, which is essentially free money. Many people on visas keep paying in because pensions can usually be accessed later, even if you move away, though the rules on transferring abroad are complicated.

## Student loan

If you studied in the UK and have a student loan, repayments appear here once you earn above the threshold. Most international students don't have UK student loans.

## Other lines you might see

- **Overtime, bonuses and allowances** added to gross pay
- **Holiday pay** for annual leave
- **Salary sacrifice** deductions for schemes like cycle-to-work or extra pension
- **Statutory sick pay** if you were off sick

## Net pay

What lands in your bank account. Check it matches the payslip.

## Keep your payslips

Save every payslip and your P60 (the end-of-year summary). You'll need them for renting, mortgages and sometimes visa applications.

## The bottom line

Take five minutes each month to check your hours, tax code and pension. Small errors add up.

*This is general information, not tax advice.*
