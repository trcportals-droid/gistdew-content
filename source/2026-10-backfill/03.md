The team's success has also shone a light on problems behind the scenes, including disputes over pay, allowances and organisation. Players and fans have called for better support for women's sport in Nigeria, and those conversations continue.

**Quick gist:** Council tax is a yearly charge from your local council for services like bin collection, street lighting and local policing. The amount depends on your home's valuation band and your council. Students, people living alone and some others can get discounts or exemptions, so always check before paying the full amount.

## Who pays

Usually the adults living in a property pay council tax, not the landlord. In shared houses rented by the room, the landlord may be responsible instead, so check your tenancy agreement.

## How much it costs

Every home in England and Scotland is in a council tax band based on its value at a fixed past date (Wales has its own bands). Your council sets the rate for each band each year. You can check your home's band on GOV.UK.

Bills usually arrive in spring for the year from April, and you can normally pay in 10 monthly instalments, or ask for 12.

## Discounts that can save you money

- **Single person discount:** if you're the only adult in the home, you get 25% off.
- **Full-time students:** a home occupied only by full-time students is usually exempt. If you live with one non-student, the bill may be reduced. Get a council tax exemption certificate from your university.
- **Student spouses and dependants:** in some cases, the non-UK spouse or dependant of a student may also be disregarded. Check with your council.
- **Council Tax Reduction:** a support scheme for people on low incomes. Eligibility for people subject to "no recourse to public funds" conditions is limited, so check carefully before applying.

## Paying the right amount

- Tell the council when you move in and move out
- Check the band is correct. If you think it's wrong, you can challenge it
- Set up a direct debit so you never miss a payment

## What happens if you don't pay

Missing payments can lead to reminders, then the council demanding the whole year's bill at once, then court action and extra costs. If you're struggling, contact the council early; they may agree a payment plan.

## Council tax and visas

Unpaid debts and court judgments can cause problems later, including with credit applications. Keeping your council tax up to date is part of building a clean financial record in the UK.

## The bottom line

Find your band, check every discount you might qualify for and set up a direct debit. It's one of the biggest household bills after rent.

*This is general information. Rules differ between England, Scotland and Wales.*

---
**Quick gist:** Children raised abroad often grow up with two identities, and that's a strength, not a problem. Parents who succeed usually make culture part of everyday life (food, language, music, stories and visits home) while also listening to their children's experiences of growing up in a different country.

## Accept that your children's childhood is different

Your children won't grow up the way you did, and that's okay. They'll have school friends from many backgrounds, different slang and different pressures. Trying to recreate your exact childhood often leads to frustration on both sides. Aim for connection, not copying.

## Speak your language at home

Language is often the first thing to fade. Many parents find these help:

- One parent speaks Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa or another language most of the time
- Pidgin or Nigerian English for everyday jokes and sayings
- Video calls with grandparents in the language
- Children's books, songs and cartoons in Nigerian languages

Even if your children answer in English, they're still learning.

## Make food a family project

Cooking jollof, egusi or puff-puff together teaches more than recipes. It creates stories, smells and memories that children carry for life.

## Tell the stories

Share stories about your childhood, your village or town, your family history and Nigeria's history. Children love hearing what you got up to at their age.

## Visit home when you can

Trips to Nigeria, even short ones, help children connect the stories to real places and people. If travel isn't possible, video calls, photos and family WhatsApp groups help keep relationships alive.

## Find your community

Churches, mosques, cultural associations and Saturday schools often run language classes, cultural days and youth groups. Being around other Nigerian children helps your children feel that their heritage is normal and shared.

## Talk about identity and racism

Children of African heritage may face racism or questions about where they're "really from". Talk openly at home, help them find words for their experience, and work with schools if problems arise. Celebrate Black History Month and Nigerian Independence Day together.

## Balance discipline and understanding

Parenting styles that were normal back home may be seen differently abroad. In the UK, for example, physical punishment of children is restricted by law, and schools take safeguarding seriously. Many parents find positive discipline, clear rules and consistent consequences work well.

## The bottom line

Children can be proudly Nigerian and comfortably British, Canadian or American at the same time. Make culture part of daily life, listen as much as you teach and enjoy watching them build their own identity.

---
**Quick gist:** Highlife is a West African music style that grew up in Ghana in the early 20th century and spread to Nigeria. It blends local rhythms and melodies with Western instruments like guitars and horns. It was the soundtrack of the independence era, and it shaped Afrobeat, Afrobeats and much of today's African pop.

## Where it started

Highlife began in what is now Ghana, where musicians combined traditional rhythms with church hymns, sea shanties and brass band music. The name is said to come from the "high life" of the elite clubs where dance bands played.

## Highlife in Nigeria

In Nigeria, highlife became the party sound of the 1950s and 1960s. Big band leaders such as Bobby Benson, Victor Olaiya and later Rex Lawson, Victor Uwaifo and Oliver De Coque became stars. Eastern Nigeria developed a strong guitar-highlife tradition, with fast, intricate guitar lines that are still loved today.

## Independence and celebration

As Ghana and Nigeria moved towards independence, highlife was everywhere: at weddings, parties and political rallies. It carried messages of pride, love, hardship and celebration.

## Fela's roots

Fela Kuti played highlife early in his career before creating Afrobeat. You can hear highlife's DNA in his work, and later in Afrobeats.

## Highlife today

Highlife never went away. It's still the sound of many weddings and owambes, and modern artists often borrow its guitar lines and melodies. Some contemporary stars mix highlife with Afrobeats, hip-hop and R&B, introducing it to a new generation.

## Where to start listening

Search streaming services for highlife classics and "best of Nigerian highlife" playlists. Ask your parents and aunties: they'll have strong opinions.

## The bottom line

If you love Afrobeats, you already love highlife, even if you didn't know it. It's the foundation much of modern West African pop is built on.

**Quick gist:** The UK Graduate visa lets eligible international students stay and work, or look for work, after completing a degree in the UK. You must apply from inside the UK before your Student visa expires, and your university must have reported that you completed your course. It doesn't require a job offer or sponsor, but it can't be extended.

## Who qualifies

Generally, you need to:

- Be in the UK on a Student visa
- Have successfully completed an eligible UK course (usually a bachelor's, master's or PhD)
- Have studied in the UK for the required period
- Have your university confirm your completion to the Home Office

Check GOV.UK for the current rules, as the length of the visa and other details have been changing.

## What you can do on it

- Work in almost any job, at any skill level
- Look for work
- Be self-employed
- Do voluntary work

You can't usually work as a professional sportsperson, and you can't access most public funds.

## How long it lasts

The length depends on your qualification and the rules in force when you apply. PhD graduates have historically been given longer than bachelor's and master's graduates. Recent policy changes have shortened the standard period, so check the latest guidance.

## How to apply

Apply online from inside the UK, before your Student visa expires. You'll pay the application fee and the Immigration Health Surcharge. You'll prove your identity through the app or an appointment, and once approved you'll get an eVisa.

## Using the time well

The Graduate visa can't be extended, so plan from day one:

- **Aim for a sponsored role.** If you want to stay long-term, target employers who hold a sponsor licence and roles that qualify for the Skilled Worker visa.
- **Check salary thresholds.** "New entrant" rules may allow a lower salary threshold for recent graduates switching from the Graduate visa.
- **Build UK experience.** Even a non-sponsored job builds your CV and references.
- **Network.** Careers services, alumni groups and professional bodies are valuable.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting until your Student visa has nearly expired
- Applying before the university has reported your completion
- Assuming any job will lead to sponsorship

## The bottom line

The Graduate visa is a valuable window, but it's limited. Plan your next step early and target sponsors from the start.

**Quick gist:** If you're a resident in Great Britain with a valid Nigerian licence, you can usually drive small vehicles for up to 12 months from when you became resident. Nigeria isn't on the list of countries whose licences can be exchanged directly, so to keep driving after that, you'll need a UK provisional licence and to pass the theory and practical tests.

## The 12-month rule

GOV.UK sets out how long you can drive on a licence from a non-EU country. For most residents with a full, valid licence, it's 12 months from the date you became resident. After that, you need a UK licence. Check GOV.UK for your exact situation.

## Why you can't just swap

The UK allows direct exchange of licences only from certain "designated countries". Nigeria isn't one of them at the time of writing, so Nigerian licence holders need to pass UK tests.

## Step 1: provisional licence

Apply online for a provisional licence through GOV.UK. You'll need your identity documents and to have been resident in Great Britain for the required time. It's cheaper online than by post.

## Step 2: theory test

The theory test has two parts: multiple-choice questions on the Highway Code and a hazard perception test using video clips. Practice apps and the official Highway Code book help a lot. Book only through the official GOV.UK service; unofficial sites charge extra.

## Step 3: lessons

Even experienced drivers from Nigeria often need lessons to learn UK test standards: mirror checks, roundabouts, lane discipline and speed limits. A few lessons with an approved driving instructor can save you failed tests.

## Step 4: practical test

Test slots can be hard to get in busy areas. Check regularly for cancellations on the official booking service. You'll drive for about 40 minutes, including independent driving and manoeuvres.

## Insurance

UK car insurance can be expensive for new arrivals with no UK driving history. Ask insurers if they'll consider your Nigerian driving record, compare quotes and consider a black box policy.

## Avoid these mistakes

- Driving on a Nigerian licence after your 12 months are up
- Driving without insurance, which carries heavy penalties
- Using unofficial test booking sites

Use your 12 months wisely: get your provisional licence, pass the theory test and start lessons early so you're not stuck when your time runs out.

sources: National Careers Service — Interview advice|https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/interview-advice
photo: office meeting room
---
**Quick gist:** Many UK employers use competency-based interviews, asking you to describe real situations where you showed a skill. The STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) helps you answer clearly. Prepare five or six strong stories, research the organisation and ask good questions at the end.

## Competency-based questions

You'll often hear questions like:

- "Tell me about a time you dealt with a difficult customer."
- "Give an example of when you worked under pressure."
- "Describe a time you disagreed with a colleague."

The interviewer wants evidence from your experience, not a general statement like "I work well under pressure".

## The STAR method

- **Situation:** set the scene briefly. Where were you, what was happening?
- **Task:** what was your responsibility?
- **Action:** what did *you* do? Use "I", not "we". This is the most important part.
- **Result:** what happened? Use numbers if you can, and what you learned.

Keep each answer to about two minutes.

## Use your Nigerian experience confidently

Experience from Nigeria is real experience. Explain the context briefly (size of organisation, number of customers or patients) and focus on what you achieved. Many interviewers value people who've worked in challenging environments.

## Research the organisation

Read the job description, the person specification and the employer's website. For the NHS and public sector, learn their values; interviewers often ask how you show them.

## Common questions to prepare

- "Tell us about yourself": a two-minute summary of your career and why this role
- "Why do you want to work here?"
- "What are your strengths and weaknesses?"
- "Where do you see yourself in five years?"

## Ask good questions

At the end, ask one or two thoughtful questions, such as about training, team structure or what success looks like in the first six months. Avoid asking about salary in the first interview unless they raise it.

## Online interviews

Many first-round interviews are on video. Test your camera and internet, sit somewhere quiet with a plain background and look at the camera when speaking.

## After the interview

Send a short thank-you email. If you're not successful, politely ask for feedback; it helps you improve for the next one.

## The bottom line

Prepare stories, practise STAR answers out loud and walk in confident about the experience you bring.

**Quick gist:** Supporting family back home is a big part of life for many Nigerians abroad. But living costs abroad are high, and many people quietly struggle to keep up with requests. A clear budget, honest conversations and fair, consistent boundaries can help you keep giving without damaging your own finances or wellbeing.

## The pressure is real

Back home, many people assume life abroad means easy money. They don't see the rent, council tax, energy bills, childcare costs and visa fees. The result is a constant stream of requests, and guilt when you say no.

## Start with your own numbers

Before you can help others, you need to know what you can afford. Write down your monthly income and essential costs: rent, bills, food, transport, savings and debt repayments. Whatever is left is what's realistically available for support.

## Decide what you'll give, in advance

Many people find it easier to set a monthly amount for family support rather than reacting to every request. For example:

- A fixed monthly amount for parents
- A yearly amount for school fees
- A small emergency fund for genuine crises

When the money for the month is used, you can honestly say so.

## Have the honest conversation

It can help to explain your real costs to close family. You don't have to share every detail, but describing rent, bills and visa fees in naira helps people understand.

## Pay directly when you can

For school fees, hospital bills or rent, paying the institution directly can make sure the money is used as intended.

## Help people stand on their own feet

Sometimes the best support is helping someone earn: paying for a course, a trade tool or start-up stock for a small business. It can reduce future requests and build dignity.

## Watch out for scams

Some "emergencies" are scams, especially messages from new numbers claiming to be relatives. Call the person directly before sending money.

## Look after yourself

Constant pressure can affect your mental health and relationships. Talk to your partner, friends or a counsellor. Your wellbeing matters too.

## The bottom line

Giving is a beautiful part of our culture, but it should be planned, not panicked. Set your budget, set your boundaries and give with a clear conscience.

**Quick gist:** The Nigerians in Diaspora Commission (NiDCOM) is a federal agency set up to engage Nigerians living abroad, protect their interests and encourage them to contribute to Nigeria's development. It works alongside Nigeria's embassies and high commissions, which remain your first stop for consular services like passports.

## Why NiDCOM was created

Nigeria has one of the largest diasporas in Africa. Nigerians abroad send home billions of dollars every year and many want to invest, volunteer or return. The commission was established by law in 2017 and started work afterwards, giving the diaspora a dedicated voice in government.

## What it does

NiDCOM's work includes:

- Engaging diaspora communities and organisations
- Advocating for Nigerians abroad who face crises, such as during conflicts or mass deportations
- Supporting evacuations and the return of distressed Nigerians, working with other agencies
- Promoting diaspora investment, skills transfer and knowledge sharing
- Organising events such as Diaspora Day

## What it doesn't do

NiDCOM isn't where you renew your passport or get consular documents. For those, go to the Nigerian embassy or high commission in your country, or the Nigeria Immigration Service portal.

## When NiDCOM might help

- If you're caught up in a crisis abroad affecting many Nigerians
- If you want to connect with diaspora investment or volunteering schemes
- If your diaspora organisation wants to engage with government

## How to contact it

NiDCOM's official website and social media accounts list contact details and announcements. Be careful of fake pages claiming to represent the commission.

## The bigger picture

The diaspora's relationship with government back home is complicated. Many Nigerians abroad want better consular services, diaspora voting and easier investment rules. Agencies like NiDCOM are part of that conversation.

## The bottom line

NiDCOM is the government's dedicated diaspora agency. For everyday consular needs, go to your embassy; for diaspora engagement and crisis advocacy, NiDCOM is the place to look.
