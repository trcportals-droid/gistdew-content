**Quick gist:** In England, landlords must check your Right to Rent, most will run references, and your deposit must be protected in a government-approved scheme. Without a UK credit history, you may be asked for a guarantor or rent in advance. Never pay a deposit before you've seen the property (in person or on a live video call) and confirmed the landlord or agent is real.

## Before you start searching

Have these ready:

- Your eVisa share code for the Right to Rent check
- Proof of income: a job offer letter, payslips or a sponsor letter
- Bank statements
- A reference from a previous landlord if you have one, even from Nigeria

Being organised makes you stand out in a competitive market.

## Right to Rent

In England, landlords must check that you have the right to rent before the tenancy starts. With an eVisa, you generate a share code on GOV.UK and give it to the landlord along with your date of birth. It's quick and free.

## Referencing and credit checks

Most agents use referencing companies that check your income, employment and credit history. New arrivals often fail the credit part simply because they don't have a UK history yet. Options include:

- **A UK guarantor** who agrees to pay if you don't
- **Rent guarantor services**, which act as a guarantor for a fee
- **Paying some rent in advance**, which some landlords accept

Read the terms carefully before agreeing to large advance payments.

## Deposits

In England, the tenancy deposit is usually capped at five weeks' rent for most tenancies. Your landlord must protect it in a government-approved scheme and give you the details within 30 days. At the end of the tenancy, if there's a dispute, the scheme's free dispute service can help.

Holding deposits (to take the property off the market) are capped at one week's rent.

## Fees you shouldn't pay

In England, the Tenant Fees Act bans most letting fees. Agents can't charge you for viewings, referencing or admin. Allowed payments include rent, the capped deposit and a few specific charges like a lost key.

## Red flags for rental scams

- The rent is far below similar properties nearby
- The "landlord" is abroad and can't show you the property
- You're asked to pay by money transfer or crypto before viewing
- Pressure to pay immediately "because many people want it"

Search the address and the agent online, and check agents belong to a redress scheme.

## Inventory and moving in

Take dated photos and videos of every room when you move in. Check the inventory carefully and report problems in writing. This protects your deposit later.

## Bills to budget for

Rent is only part of it. Budget for council tax, energy, water, internet and a TV licence if you watch live TV. Ask the agent what's included.

## The bottom line

Prepare your documents, understand your rights, protect your deposit and trust your instincts when something feels too good to be true.

*This is general information for England; rules differ in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.*

**Quick gist:** To work as a registered nurse in the UK, you need to join the Nursing and Midwifery Council (NMC) register. For nurses trained outside the UK, that usually means meeting English language requirements, passing a computer-based test (CBT) and then a practical exam called the OSCE. Many NHS trusts recruit internationally and sponsor the Health and Care Worker visa.

## Step 1: check your eligibility

The NMC sets the rules on qualifications, practice experience and English. Check its website for the current requirements for internationally educated nurses before you spend money on tests.

## Step 2: English language

You'll usually need to show English through an approved test, such as IELTS Academic or the Occupational English Test (OET), at the required scores. In some cases, other evidence like recent practice in an English-speaking country may be accepted. The NMC's guidance sets out the options.

## Step 3: the CBT

The computer-based test checks your nursing knowledge. It's taken at approved test centres, including in Nigeria. Many candidates use practice materials and study groups to prepare.

## Step 4: the job offer

Many NHS trusts and some private healthcare providers recruit internationally. You'll typically be offered a job as a band 3 or 4 while you finish the registration steps, then move to band 5 once you join the register. Search NHS Jobs and trust websites, and be very careful with agents. Genuine NHS recruitment shouldn't require you to pay huge fees to an agent.

## Step 5: the visa

Most international nurses come on the Health and Care Worker visa, which has lower fees than the standard Skilled Worker visa and an exemption from the Immigration Health Surcharge. Your employer issues a Certificate of Sponsorship.

## Step 6: the OSCE

The Objective Structured Clinical Examination is a practical test of your clinical skills. Most international nurses take it in the UK after arriving, and employers usually provide preparation and pay for the first attempt.

## Step 7: join the register

Once you pass the OSCE and the NMC completes its checks, you join the register, get your PIN and can work as a registered nurse.

## Watch out for

- **Agents charging huge fees** or asking you to sign repayment contracts you don't understand
- **Unethical recruitment:** the UK has a code of practice on international recruitment of health workers. Read it.
- **Contract repayment clauses:** some employers ask you to repay relocation costs if you leave early. Understand the terms before signing.

## Life in the NHS

Nigerian nurses are a big and respected part of the NHS workforce. Join professional networks and associations for Nigerian nurses in the UK; they're a great source of support and advice.

## The bottom line

The route is long but well-trodden. Go through official channels, prepare well for each test and read every contract carefully.

**Quick gist:** At the 1996 Olympic Games in Atlanta, Nigeria's under-23 football team, nicknamed the Dream Team, became the first African side to win Olympic football gold. ## A team full of talent

The squad was built mainly of young players under 23, with a few allowed overage players. Many of them were already playing in Europe or would go on to big careers there. Names like Nwankwo Kanu, Jay-Jay Okocha, Celestine Babayaro and Taribo West became household names.

## The road to the final

Nigeria came through the group stage and beat Mexico in the quarter-finals. Then came Brazil, a team packed with future world stars. Nigeria were behind but fought back, and Kanu scored the decisive goal in extra time. For many Nigerians, that night is still one of the greatest of their lives.

## The final against Argentina

In the final, Nigeria again fell behind and again refused to give up. Emmanuel Amunike scored a late winner to make it 3–2, and Nigeria were Olympic champions. Celebrations spread from Lagos to every Nigerian community abroad.

## Why it mattered so much

The win showed the world that African football could beat the very best. It came at a difficult time politically in Nigeria, and for a moment it gave the whole country something to celebrate together. Four years later, Cameroon won Olympic gold too, confirming that 1996 was no fluke for African football.

## Where are they now?

Many of the 1996 players went on to long careers in Europe, and several are now coaches, pundits and administrators. Kanu, who survived serious heart surgery after the Games, set up a foundation to help children with heart conditions.

## The legacy for the diaspora

Ask any Nigerian parent in London or Houston about Atlanta 1996 and you'll probably get a story about exactly where they were when Amunike scored. It's part of the shared memory that connects Nigerians at home and abroad.

## Nigeria at the Olympics today

Nigeria has gone on to win Olympic medals in other sports, including athletics and wrestling, and the national football teams continue to qualify regularly. But the 1996 gold remains the standard every new generation is measured against.

## The bottom line

Atlanta 1996 wasn't just a football win. It was proof of what Nigerian talent can do on the world stage, and it still inspires young players today.

**Quick gist:** The Bank Verification Number (BVN) is an 11-digit number that links your identity to every bank account you hold in Nigeria. If you already had a Nigerian account before you left, you have one. Nigerians abroad who need a BVN can often enrol through certain banks' diaspora services or partners abroad. Treat your BVN like a password and never share it with strangers.

## What the BVN does

The BVN was introduced by the Central Bank of Nigeria to reduce fraud and make it easier for banks to verify customers. It's tied to your fingerprints and facial image. Every Nigerian bank account must be linked to a BVN, and you can't open a new one without it.

## Already have one but forgot it?

If you still have a Nigerian phone line linked to your bank account, you can usually retrieve your BVN through your bank's official USSD code or app. Your bank's customer service can also help after verifying your identity.

## Getting a BVN from abroad

Some Nigerian banks offer diaspora accounts and BVN enrolment for customers living abroad, sometimes through partner locations or their own offices in countries like the UK. The process usually involves:

- Applying through the bank's diaspora banking service
- Providing your Nigerian passport and proof of address abroad
- Completing biometric capture at an approved location

Options vary between banks, so contact your bank or check its official website.

## Why it matters for diaspora life

- **Supporting family:** if you send money to your own Nigerian account first, you need it active and BVN-linked.
- **Investments:** many Nigerian investment platforms and pension products require a BVN.
- **Property and business:** buying land or running a business back home usually involves a Nigerian account.

## Keeping your BVN safe

BVN fraud is real. Fraudsters may call pretending to be from your bank or the CBN, saying your BVN needs "updating" or your account will be blocked.

- Your bank will never ask for your PIN, password or OTP by phone, text or social media
- Don't click links in unexpected messages
- If in doubt, call your bank using the number on its official website

## Updating your details

If your name, phone number or date of birth needs correcting, go through your bank. Changes can require documents such as an affidavit or marriage certificate, so start early if you need things fixed before a big transaction.

## The bottom line

If you bank in Nigeria, your BVN is your financial identity. Know it, keep it secure and use your bank's official channels for any changes.

## The home-video boom

The film often credited with launching modern Nollywood is *Living in Bondage* (1992), a thriller about a man who sacrifices his wife for wealth. It was made on a small budget and sold on video cassette, and it was a huge hit. Producers soon realised that audiences wanted stories in their own languages and settings, and an industry was born.

## Quantity, speed and stories people knew

In the early years, films were shot in days, not months. Markets like Idumota in Lagos and Onitsha became distribution hubs. The stories covered family, money, faith, witchcraft, love and betrayal: themes that felt real to audiences across Africa and the Caribbean.

For Nigerians abroad, Nollywood was a lifeline. African shops in London, Houston and Toronto rented and sold Nollywood DVDs, and families gathered to watch them together. Later, YouTube channels and dedicated streaming apps brought thousands of films to the diaspora at the click of a button.

## New Nollywood

From around the 2010s, a new wave of filmmakers raised production values, focused on cinema releases and told new kinds of stories. Comedies, romances and crime dramas began breaking box-office records in Nigeria. Big-budget remakes and sequels of classic titles, including *Living in Bondage: Breaking Free*, connected old and new audiences.

## Streaming goes global

Global platforms started commissioning Nigerian originals and licensing Nollywood films, putting them in front of viewers in dozens of countries. Nigerian series and films have topped charts on these services in multiple regions.

## Challenges

Nollywood still faces real problems: piracy, funding, limited cinema screens and the pressure to make content quickly. Filmmakers continue to push for better pay, stronger copyright protection and more investment.

- Watch on official platforms instead of pirated uploads
- Go to the cinema when Nigerian films get UK or North American releases
- Follow and share the work of filmmakers and actors you love

## The bottom line

From market-stall VHS tapes to global streaming, Nollywood is one of Nigeria's greatest cultural exports, and the diaspora has been part of the journey from the start.

**Quick gist:** Express Entry is an online system Canada uses to manage applications for permanent residence from skilled workers. You create a profile, get a Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score, and wait in a pool. Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) holds regular draws and invites the highest-scoring or targeted candidates to apply.

## The three main programmes

Express Entry covers:

- **Federal Skilled Worker Program**, for skilled workers with foreign experience
- **Canadian Experience Class**, for people with skilled work experience in Canada
- **Federal Skilled Trades Program**, for qualified tradespeople

Provincial Nominee Programs can also use Express Entry. A provincial nomination adds a large number of points.

## How the CRS score works

Your CRS score is based on factors like:

- Age
- Education
- Language ability in English and/or French
- Work experience, especially in Canada
- Your spouse's education, language and experience, if applicable
- Extras like a provincial nomination, French skills or a sibling in Canada

Language test results often make the biggest difference you can control. Many Nigerian applicants improve their score significantly by retaking IELTS or CELPIP to reach higher bands, or by learning French.

## Draws: general and category-based

IRCC holds draws throughout the year. Some invite the top candidates overall, others target specific groups, such as Canadian Experience Class, French speakers or particular occupations like healthcare. Cut-off scores change from draw to draw, so look at recent results rather than old ones.

## After you're invited

An Invitation to Apply gives you a limited time to submit a full application with documents such as:

- Educational Credential Assessment (ECA)
- Police certificates from countries where you've lived
- Medical exam results
- Proof of funds, unless you're exempt
- Reference letters for your work experience

Missing or inconsistent documents are a common cause of refusal.

## Proof of funds

If you're applying under the Federal Skilled Worker or Skilled Trades programmes without a valid Canadian job offer, you must show settlement funds. The amounts are updated each year, so check IRCC's current table.

## Beware of fake consultants

Only licensed consultants (members of the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants) or lawyers can charge you for immigration advice. No one can guarantee an invitation.

## The bottom line

Maximise what you can control, especially language scores, keep your documents ready and follow official IRCC updates on draws.

**Quick gist:** At the time of writing, Nigerians living abroad cannot vote in Nigerian elections from overseas. To vote, you must be registered and physically present at your polling unit in Nigeria. Bills to allow diaspora voting have been proposed many times but would need changes to Nigeria's laws and, according to many legal experts, the constitution.

## How voting works now

Nigeria's electoral law requires voters to register with the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) and vote in person at the polling unit where they're registered. There's no postal vote and no voting at embassies. So a Nigerian living in London who wants to vote has to travel home for election day.

## Why diaspora voting keeps coming up

Nigerians abroad send billions of dollars home every year, support families and invest in property and businesses. Many argue that people who contribute so much should have a say in who governs. Diaspora groups have campaigned on this for years.

## What would need to change

Supporters of diaspora voting say Nigeria would need to:

- Amend the electoral law to allow voting outside Nigeria
- Possibly amend the constitution
- Set up systems for registration abroad, such as at embassies and consulates
- Decide how to handle security, logistics and cost across many countries

Several bills have been introduced in the National Assembly over the years, but none has completed the full process into law at the time of writing.

## The arguments against, or for caution

Critics and cautious voices raise concerns about:

- The cost of running elections in many countries
- The risk of fraud or disputes over results
- The capacity of embassies to handle registration and voting
- Whether to prioritise improving elections at home first

## How other African countries do it

Several African countries allow some form of diaspora voting, often at embassies. Supporters point to these examples; critics point to low turnout and high costs in some of them.

## What you can do now

- Follow NiDCOM and diaspora organisations that campaign on the issue
- Engage with your representatives in Nigeria
- If you'll be home during an election, check INEC's rules on registration and transferring your voter registration

## The bottom line

For now, voting means being in Nigeria on election day. Diaspora voting remains a live debate, and any change would need new laws and a lot of preparation.

**Quick gist:** UK recruiters expect a clean, two-page CV with no photo, no date of birth, no marital status and no religion. Lead with a short profile, list experience in reverse order, and focus on achievements with numbers rather than long lists of duties. Tailor it for every job.

## What to leave out

These are common on Nigerian CVs but not expected in the UK, and some can even work against you:

- **Photo**
- **Date of birth and age**
- **Marital status, number of children, religion, state of origin**
- **Full home address** (your town is enough)
- **"References available on request"**, which is assumed

UK equality law aims to stop discrimination based on things like age and religion, so recruiters prefer CVs without them.

## The structure that works

1. **Name and contact details:** phone, email, town, LinkedIn.
2. **Personal profile:** three or four lines on who you are, your key skills and what you're looking for.
3. **Work experience:** most recent first, with job title, employer, location and dates.
4. **Education and qualifications:** most recent first. Add the UK equivalent if helpful.
5. **Skills:** software, languages, certifications.

Keep it to two pages for most roles.

## Achievements, not duties

Compare these:

- *Duties:* "Responsible for managing customer accounts."
- *Achievement:* "Managed 120 customer accounts and grew renewals by 18% in one year."

Numbers make your impact clear. Think about money saved, targets beaten, people trained and processes improved.

## Explaining Nigerian experience

UK employers may not know your former employer. Add a short line such as "one of Nigeria's largest telecoms providers" or "a 200-bed teaching hospital". Mention international standards or clients if relevant.

## Tailor for every application

Read the job description and match its keywords. Many employers use software that scans CVs for relevant terms. If the advert says "stakeholder management", use that phrase, as long as it's true.

## Right to work

You don't need to put your visa details on your CV, but you can add a line such as "Full right to work in the UK" or "Require sponsorship". Being clear saves time for both sides.

## Cover letters and applications

Many UK jobs, especially in the NHS and public sector, use application forms with a "supporting statement". Treat it seriously: use the person specification as a checklist and give examples for each point.

## Final checks

- Use UK spelling (organise, programme, CV)
- Save as PDF unless asked otherwise
- Use a professional email address
- Ask a friend in the UK to read it

## The bottom line

A clear, tailored, achievement-focused CV gets interviews. Strip out what UK recruiters don't need and show what you've delivered.
